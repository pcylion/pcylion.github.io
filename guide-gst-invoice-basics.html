GST & Compliance

GST invoice basics for small shopkeepers

Not every shop needs to worry about GST invoicing — and the ones that do usually need far less than they assume. Here's a plain-language rundown.

Do you even need to charge GST?

If your total annual turnover is below the GST registration threshold (₹40 lakh for most goods sellers, ₹20 lakh for services, with lower limits in a few special-category states), you generally don't need to register for GST or charge it at all. Many small shopkeepers fall under this and can stop reading here — a plain, itemized bill without GST is completely fine. This isn't tax advice for your specific situation, though — if you're close to the threshold or unsure, a local tax consultant can confirm quickly.

If you are registered, what actually has to be on the bill?

  • Your GSTIN (the 15-character registration number)
  • A unique, sequential invoice number
  • The invoice date
  • Your name and address, and the customer's (for larger transactions)
  • A description of goods/services, quantity, and unit price
  • The GST rate and amount charged, shown separately from the price
  • The total invoice value

That's the core of it — the rest of what GST software adds (HSN/SAC codes, e-invoicing, etc.) mostly matters at larger turnover levels, not for a typical small shop just starting to register.

The most common mistake: hiding tax inside the price

If you're registered and charging GST, it needs its own line — "Subtotal ₹460, GST (5%) ₹23, Total ₹483" — rather than just quietly folding it into a higher per-item price. Hiding it isn't just poor practice, it also means you can't prove to a customer (or to a tax officer) what was actually charged as tax.

Keep every bill, even the small ones

If you're GST-registered, every invoice you issue is part of your filing record. It's far easier to keep a running digital record from day one than to try to reconstruct three months of paper bills right before a filing deadline.

One thing worth repeating: this is general guidance, not filing advice

GST rules do vary by state, category of goods, and your specific turnover — this guide covers the general shape of things, not a substitute for checking with a tax professional about your specific shop.
