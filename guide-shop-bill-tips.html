Billing

How to make a professional bill for your shop

A bill is often the only "paperwork" a customer ever sees from your shop. A few small details are the difference between a bill that looks trustworthy and one that raises questions.

1. Put your shop name and contact details at the top, always

Every bill should clearly show your shop's name, address, and a phone number — even for a regular customer. If a customer ever needs to return an item, confirm a price, or ask about a warranty weeks later, the bill is what they'll dig out. A bill with no way to reach you back looks like it came from nowhere.

2. Number your bills, and never reuse a number

A simple running number (0001, 0002, 0003...) does two things: it tells a customer this is a real business that keeps records, and it gives you an easy way to find any bill later — for a refund, a dispute, or your own year-end totals. Skipping numbers or reusing an old number after a mistake is a common habit that makes bookkeeping messy later; it's better to just void the mistaken bill and move to the next number.

3. Show quantity, unit, and rate separately — not just a total

"₹460" tells a customer nothing. "5 kg of rice at ₹92/kg = ₹460" tells them exactly what they paid for and lets them check your math themselves, which builds trust. It also protects you: if a customer comes back a week later claiming they were overcharged, an itemized bill settles the question in ten seconds.

4. Be upfront about discounts and tax, don't bury them

If you're giving a discount, show it as its own line rather than just quietly lowering a price — customers notice being given a discount, and it reads as generosity rather than a random number. Same with any tax you're charging: show it separately so the final total is never a surprise.

5. Date every bill, and keep a copy

A dated bill protects both sides in any later disagreement about price, warranty period, or whether an item was even purchased there. Keeping a copy — on paper or digitally — means you're not relying on memory if a question comes up months later.

6. Mark clearly whether it's been paid in full

If a customer pays partially or asks to pay later, write that down on the bill itself, not just in your head. "Paid ₹300 of ₹500, ₹200 due" avoids any confusion about what's still owed — for both of you.
