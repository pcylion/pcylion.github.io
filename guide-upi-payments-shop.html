Accepting UPI payments safely in a small shop

UPI has made getting paid faster than ever — it's also created new, very specific scams aimed at shopkeepers. Here's how to take UPI payments with confidence.

The golden rule: receiving money never requires you to enter your PIN

This is the single most important thing to know. Your UPI PIN is only ever needed when you are sending or approving a payment — never when someone is paying you. If any message, call, or "collect request" asks you to enter your PIN to "receive" a refund, cashback, or payment, it is always a scam. No exceptions.

Watch out for fake "payment received" screenshots

A common trick: a customer shows you a screenshot claiming they've already paid, and asks you to hand over goods before you've actually checked. Screenshots can be faked easily. Always confirm the money has actually landed — check your own bank SMS or UPI app notification — before completing the sale, especially for larger amounts.

Be suspicious of "collect requests," not just payment links

A UPI "collect request" is a request sent to you asking you to pay someone else — it looks similar to an incoming payment notification but does the opposite. Scammers rely on shopkeepers approving these quickly without reading carefully. Always read what a UPI notification actually says before approving anything — "You will pay ₹X" is completely different from "You will receive ₹X."

Double-check the UPI ID before sharing it widely

If you display your UPI ID on a due reminder, a printed bill, or a QR code, make sure it's typed correctly and tested with a small transaction first. A single typo in a UPI ID can send money to a stranger's account, and UPI transfers usually can't be reversed once completed.

Never share OTPs, even with someone claiming to be from your bank or UPI app

Banks and UPI providers do not call asking for your OTP to "verify," "activate," or "reverse" a transaction. Any call like this — however official it sounds — is a scam. Hang up and, if worried, call your bank back using the number printed on your own card or passbook.

Keep a record of every UPI payment against the actual bill

It's easy to lose track of which UPI payment matched which sale, especially on a busy day. Noting the payment against the bill at the time — not from memory later — avoids disputes and makes it much easier to spot a missing payment early.
