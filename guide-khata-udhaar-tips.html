Credit & Dues

Managing udhaar (credit): how to give it without losing money

Giving credit to regular customers is normal in most Indian shops — the problem is never the udhaar itself, it's not tracking it properly.

Write it down the moment it happens, not later

The single biggest source of udhaar losses isn't dishonest customers — it's a shopkeeper who meant to write it down and got busy. If a due isn't recorded the same day, on the same bill, it's very easy to forget the exact amount, or forget it entirely. Record the due against the bill itself, at the time of the sale, every time.

Track dues by customer, not just by bill

A single due of ₹200 doesn't look serious. But if the same customer has ₹200 due on five separate bills you never added up, that's ₹1,000 you didn't realize was outstanding. Keep one running balance per customer, not a scattered pile of individual due-bills, so you always know the real total.

Set a mental (or written) limit per customer

Most successful shopkeepers who give credit have an informal cap — a point where they'll say "let's clear the last one first" before adding more. This isn't about distrust; it's just good practice, the same way a bank sets a credit limit. A customer who's a regular and pays reliably will understand a polite "let's settle up a bit before the next one."

Send reminders before the amount grows, not after

A small, polite reminder at ₹300 owed is a normal nudge. The same reminder at ₹3,000 owed after six months feels like a confrontation — for both of you. Reminding early and often, in a friendly tone, keeps the relationship comfortable and keeps amounts from snowballing into something awkward to bring up.

Make paying back easy — remove every excuse

"I'll pay next time I'm at the shop" often becomes months of forgetting. If you can send a reminder straight to their phone with your UPI ID attached, a customer can clear their due from home in ten seconds, with no need to visit, call, or ask for your bank details. The easier it is to pay, the faster people actually do it.

Record partial payments immediately, and keep the note visible

If someone pays half of what they owe, update the balance right away — don't wait to "do it later." A customer who pays ₹200 of a ₹500 due and later gets asked for the full ₹500 again (because it wasn't updated) will rightly feel cheated, even if it was just a bookkeeping slip.
